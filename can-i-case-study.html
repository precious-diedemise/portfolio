01 / Product evolution

The idea changed. The goal didn't.

The original idea was simple: test financial decisions before committing. Building a useful comparison revealed that the tool first needed a clearer picture of the user's money.

01

The question

What happens if I pay this debt, save this amount, or make this purchase?

02

The foundation

Organize money on hand, obligations, savings, subscriptions, and expected income.

03

The comparison

Explore different allocations and understand what each choice changes.

The product decision: Organizing comes first, not because Can I? was originally a budgeting app, but because financial comparisons need context.

03 / Product decisions

What changed, and why?

Product work is not just about adding features. These are the trade-offs shaping the experience.

Journey design

Organize before comparing

Reasoning: A comparison without bills, debts, and money on hand can overlook important constraints.

Recommendation logic

Align options with the goal

Reasoning: If a user intends to allocate an amount toward debt, an option that leaves the entire amount unused is not meaningful.

User experience

Shorter, clearer screens

Reasoning: More explanation is not always more helpful. Users need to see what a choice does without reading a wall of text.

Financial context

Separate incoming from available

Reasoning: Money expected later is useful to plan around, but it shouldn't automatically be treated as cash available today.

04 / Early feedback & iteration

You test. You learn. You change things.

Early testers and design reviews surfaced several recurring issues. Expand an item to see the product response. These are feedback themes, not direct quotations or claims of completed fixes.

Some labels didn't make sense

Terms like “Protected” were not immediately intuitive to users.

DESIGN RESPONSE
Prefer familiar language such as “Savings” and explain financial categories only when needed.

Debt comparisons left money untouched

Some suggested options didn't align with the stated goal of using money to reduce debt.

DESIGN RESPONSE
Explore goal-aware allocations and show how the full chosen amount is distributed.

Too many words and inputs

Long descriptions and overlapping choices could make the experience harder to understand.

DESIGN RESPONSE
Simplify language, remove duplication, and guide users through smaller steps.

The financial picture felt incomplete

Feedback raised questions about incoming pay, recurring subscriptions, unforeseen expenses, and saved activity.

DESIGN RESPONSE
Assess which organizing features are necessary before expanding the comparison experience.

05 / What's next

Still building, still learning.

The next challenge is keeping the financial picture useful without turning a simple decision tool into a complicated system.

Usability

Make comparisons easier to read

Reduce unnecessary copy and make allocation results more immediately understandable.

Organization

Clarify what belongs in the overview

Continue evaluating subscriptions, incoming money, recurring obligations, and activity history.

Validation

Keep testing assumptions

Observe where users hesitate, revise the flow, and test whether changes improve clarity.
