Product development · Financial decision-making

Can I? Before I do.

I wanted a way to see how a money decision might play out before actually making it. That question became Can I?, a student-focused tool that is still taking shape.

Work in progress · Actively evolving

An independent product project. This page documents decisions and ongoing testing, not a finished commercial product.

01 / The product evolution

The idea changed. The goal didn't.

Can I? started as a way to compare financial decisions. As I worked through the experience, organizing the numbers became the necessary first step. You need to understand your money before comparing what to do with it.

01

Start with a question

What happens if I pay this debt, save this amount, or make this purchase?

02

Organize the picture

Bring available money, obligations, savings, subscriptions, and expected income into view.

03

Compare the choices

Test different allocations and see how each decision changes the financial picture.

02 / Product decisions

Designing for real decisions.

Decision 01

Context before comparison

Financial inputs come first, so a comparison isn't made in isolation.

Decision 02

Make the options meaningful

If the goal is to reduce debt, suggested allocations should reflect that goal, not arbitrarily leave all the money untouched.

Decision 03

Reduce unnecessary effort

Shorter labels, fewer confusing choices, and sample numbers to make the experience easier to explore.

Decision 04

Keep the financial picture connected

Explore how money on hand, incoming funds, bills, debts, and planned spending relate to one another.

03 / Testing and iteration

You test. You learn. You change things.

Early testing surfaced questions about the language, what counts as available money, and whether suggested allocations actually respected a user's goal. Those questions are shaping the next iterations.

Confusing financial labels

What surfaced: Some terms, including “Protected,” weren't intuitive.
Design response: Use clearer language such as “Savings” and simplify prompts.

Options that miss the goal

What surfaced: Debt-focused comparisons could suggest keeping money available instead of paying down debt.
Design response: Rework suggested allocations around the user's stated goal.

Too much to process

What surfaced: Wordy screens and numerous inputs could make decisions harder.
Design response: Shorter copy, more focused steps, and fewer unnecessary fields.

Missing financial context

What surfaced: Feedback called for better handling of recurring bills, subscriptions, unexpected expenses, and incoming money.
Design response: Evaluate how these elements fit into the organizing flow without overcomplicating it.

These are summarized themes from development conversations and early feedback, not verbatim testimonial quotes. Responses and revisions remain ongoing.

04 / What's next

Still figuring things out.

The next challenge is keeping the financial overview useful without making the tool feel like another complicated budgeting app.
